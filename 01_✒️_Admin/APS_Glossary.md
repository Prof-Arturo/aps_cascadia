## ENTRY 001

# Periphenomena

**(n. pl.)** /ˌpɛr.i.fɪˈnɑː.mɪ.nə/ — stress on the third syllable, following *phenomena*. British English: /ˌpɛr.i.fɪˈnɒm.ɪ.nə/.
**Singular:** periphenomenon /ˌpɛr.i.fɪˈnɑː.mɪ.nɒn/
**Adjective:** periphenomenal
**Adverb:** periphenomenally *(rare; avoid in project prose)*

### 1 · Etymology

From Greek **περί** (*peri-*) — "around, near, at the edge, surrounding" — plus **φαινόμενα** (*phainomena*), plural of *phainomenon*, "that which appears."

Literally: **appearances at the edge.**

### 2 · Definition

> **Periphenomena** are experiences at the edge of the phenomenal — real, structured, and reportable, occurring adjacent to and continuous with ordinary experience without being fully accommodated by conventional scientific, psychological, or cultural explanatory frameworks.

*(Mission Statement §2.0, verbatim.)*

**What "real" is doing in that sentence.** It qualifies the *experience*, not a mechanism behind it. Real people had real experiences and described them consistently. That is an empirical claim the archive can stand behind. Whether any given experience corresponds to an external physical event is a separate question, and the definition deliberately leaves it open.

This is the distinction the v3.0 entry lost. See the reconciliation note.

### 3 · What the prefix rules out

The term was chosen over four available alternatives, each carrying a commitment the APS does not make.

| Term | Prefix | Commitment the APS declines |
|---|---|---|
| **Paranormal** | *para-* — beyond, alongside | That the experience belongs to a class operating by rules other than those governing ordinary reality. |
| **Supernatural** | *super-* — above | That the experience transcends nature. Directly conflicts with Methodological Agnosticism. |
| **Epiphenomenal** | *epi-* — upon, over | A *causal* position: real, structured, downstream of a process, not altering the chain that produced it. The Theoretical Framework treats synchronicity this way. **Not a synonym** — one term describes causal position, the other phenomenological position. |
| **Anomalous** | — deviating from the regular | Accurate but flat. Captures deviation from expectation; misses **continuity**. A moment of missing time is not a rupture in the everyday — it is a peculiarity at the edge of it. |

**Periphenomenal** makes a narrower and more modest claim: these experiences occur at the periphery of the phenomenal, in the boundary zone where ordinary experience begins to admit patterns, timings, presences, and sensory events that resist easy accommodation.

They are not transcendent. They are not causally inert. They are not merely irregular.

**They are adjacent.**

### 4 · The third position

Two framings dominate discussion of this material, and the project holds neither.

- **Paranormal framings** propose that a distinct order of reality exists, awaiting discovery.
- **Reductive framings** propose that these experiences dissolve on examination into ordinary cognitive, cultural, or physiological processes.

The APS holds that experiences at the edge of the phenomenal **are real, are patterned, and are proper objects of documentation and study** — without prejudging whether they will ultimately be accommodated by expansions of existing frameworks, by frameworks not yet developed, by something else, or by some combination.

That last clause is the whole of the project's epistemic position, and it is load-bearing. Any formulation that closes it — in either direction — is off-canon.

### 6 · Usage

**In protocol and analyst prose:**

> "The witness reported a periphenomenal encounter involving acoustic distortion and altered duration."

> "Three periphenomena were logged at this location across two decades."

> "Type IX preserves the witness's own framework as the primary frame for the periphenomenon reported."

**In witness-facing material: not at all.**

The Iceberg Method applies. *Periphenomena* is internal vocabulary. A witness reading the intake portal encounters "experiences that don't have an easy explanation" — not the project's technical noun. The word appears in witness-facing text only where the project's name requires it, and the name is never glossed in the register of a definition.

**Agreement.** Plural: *periphenomena are*. Singular: *a periphenomenon is*. The *-a* ending is plural; "a periphenomena" is an error and appears in some v3.0-era drafts.

**Do not write** *periphenomenal event* where *periphenomenon* will do, and do not use *periphenomenal* as an intensifier — the resemblance to *phenomenal* in its colloquial sense ("outstanding") is a live misreading, and the two words mean nothing like each other.

See `voice_style_guide.md` §2 (Iceberg Method) and §3 (terminology swaps).

## Reconciliation note — v3.0 → v1.0

The v3.0 entry made three claims Mission Statement §2.0 declines. All three are removed above. They are recorded here rather than silently dropped, because versions of them have circulated and may appear in older drafts.

### 1 · "Physically real"

> **v3.0:** "Unlike 'Paranormal' (which implies a violation of natural law), Periphenomenal suggests an occurrence that is **physically real** but transient or elusive."

This asserts physical reality. §2.0 asserts the reality of the *experience* and leaves the mechanism open. The v3.0 formulation is an improvement on *paranormal* in one direction and an equivalent error in the other — it trades one ontological commitment for its opposite rather than declining both.

### 2 · "Struggle to categorize"

> **v3.0:** "Periphenomena are not necessarily 'impossible' events, but rather 'unprocessed' events—data that **the human brain or current scientific instruments struggle to categorize**."

This locates the difficulty entirely in the observer and the instruments — which is a reductive commitment: the phenomena are ordinary, and only our processing of them falls short. §2.0 explicitly holds open that existing frameworks may need expansion, that frameworks not yet developed may be required, or that the answer lies elsewhere. "Not yet processed" quietly forecloses the middle option.

### 3 · "The peripheral natural"

> **v3.0:** "We do not study the supernatural; we study **the peripheral natural**."

Memorable, and it asserts that the phenomena are natural. Same error as (1), more quotably — which makes it more likely to end up in an interview and harder to retract.

**Retired. Suggested replacement, for the same rhetorical slot:**

> *"We don't study the supernatural. We study what happens at the edge of the ordinary."*

This keeps the shape and the contrast while committing only to what §2.0 commits to. It is offered rather than adopted — the phrase has not been reviewed for the press kit, and `APS_Elevator_Pitches.md` §5 handles the skeptical question without needing it.

### Also changed

- **Five-type table → nine-type.** The v3.0 entry's "APS Classification (The 5 Types)" heading was followed by a table that had drifted from the taxonomy and used the retired 🛌 glyph for Type IV.
- **Pronunciation.** v3.0 gave /pɛri.fɪnɒmɪnə/ with no stress mark and the British vowel. Stress added, American vowel given first, British noted.
- **Inflection added.** Adverb form, agreement rules, and the *phenomenal*-as-intensifier caution are new.
- **Usage scope added.** v3.0 gave two example sentences and no statement that the word is internal-only. That omission is the kind that produces a witness-facing document using the project's theoretical vocabulary.

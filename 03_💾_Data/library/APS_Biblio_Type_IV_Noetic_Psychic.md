## Scope

Interior experience: anomalous dreams, premonition, telepathic impression, synchronicity, and the sense of knowing something one had no way to know. Also the **noetic quality** itself — the felt certainty of having apprehended something true, which James identified and which recurs across every type in the taxonomy.

## Boundary notes

**💭 IV vs 👁 VI — the most consequential boundary in the taxonomy.** Type IV is an impression located *within* the witness. Type VI is a presence located *outside* the witness, including at sleep onset or waking. A figure at the bedside is **Type VI**, not Type IV, however altered the witness's state.

This file's glyph records that correction. v3.0 marked Type IV with 🛌, which pulled bedside-figure reports toward Type IV on the strength of the setting rather than the phenomenology, and competed directly with Type VI. The glyph is now 💭 and the retired 🛌 should not be reintroduced — the cost was miscoded cases, not merely a redundant icon.

**💭 IV vs 🌐 IX** — a premonition the witness frames as ancestral communication, prophecy, or a tradition's recognised form of knowing is **Type IX primary**, Type IV secondary.

**Register caution.** This type generates the strongest pull toward the project's prohibited speech acts. A witness describing a premonition that preceded a death is making a claim an analyst must neither confirm nor correct. Record it; code it; do not resolve it. See `voice_style_guide.md` §5.

## Reading order

**James 1902** (the "Mysticism" lectures), then **Sacks 2012** for the baseline, then **Adler 2011** as the model of how the two combine.

## The noetic quality

### James, William — *The Varieties of Religious Experience: A Study in Human Nature*

New York: Longmans, Green, 1902. Modern ed. ISBN 9780140172670. OCLC [39074759](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/39074759).

James identifies **noetic quality** — the immediate, unargued sense of having apprehended truth — as one of the four marks of mystical states, alongside ineffability, transiency, and passivity. The type takes its name from this. Still the best description of *why* witnesses across all nine types report certainty disproportionate to the evidence available to them, and why that certainty should be recorded as phenomenological data rather than treated as a credibility problem.

## Mechanism baseline

### Sacks, Oliver — *Hallucinations*

New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 2012. ISBN 9780307957898. OCLC [785555474](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/785555474).

Sacks surveys hallucinatory experience across neurological causes — Charles Bonnet syndrome, hypnagogia, migraine aura, bereavement, sensory deprivation — with unusual care for what the experiences are *like*. Held as the type's mechanism baseline, and valuable for a particular reason: Sacks establishes that hallucination is common, is not a mark of pathology, and does not reduce the experience's significance to the person. That is a finding an archive can use directly in debriefing.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 👁 VI

### Blackmore, Susan — *Beyond the Body: An Investigation of Out-of-the-Body Experiences*

London: Heinemann, 1982. ISBN 9780434357708. OCLC [8262686](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/8262686).

Blackmore's psychological and experimental treatment of out-of-body experience, arguing for cognitive and body-schema mechanisms over external explanations — written by someone who began with the opposite expectation and says so. The methodological candour is part of its value.

`KEY` · `frame: mechanism`

### McNally, Richard J. — *Remembering Trauma*

Cambridge, MA: Belknap Press of Harvard University Press, 2003. ISBN 9780674011746. OCLC [52990545](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/52990545).

How traumatic memory is encoded, retrieved, and reconstructed, including the conditions under which confident recollection forms around events that did not occur as recalled. Load-bearing for the APS at the level of **procedure** rather than conclusion: McNally's work on suggestibility and retrieval is the strongest argument for the project's refusal to use leading questions, and against any form of memory-recovery technique in debriefing. Read alongside the Methodology file.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 📐 PROTOCOL

## Culture and mechanism together

### Adler, Shelley R. — *Sleep Paralysis: Night-mares, Nocebos, and the Mind-Body Connection*

Studies in Medical Anthropology. New Brunswick, NJ: Rutgers University Press, 2011. ISBN 9780813548869 (pbk.); 9780813548852 (hbk.). [Publisher](https://www.rutgersuniversitypress.org/sleep-paralysis/9780813548869)

Adler's study of Hmong *dab tsog* deaths connects physiology and cultural interpretation without reducing either to the other, and makes the case that a culturally held expectation can have physical consequence. The model this type should be read through: the mechanism and the meaning are both real, and establishing one does not dispose of the other.

**Citation correction.** v3.0 gave this as "New York: Rutgers University Press, 2011. ISBN 9780190616459" with an Oxford University Press link. The place and the identifiers were both wrong — 9780190616459 is not this book, and Rutgers is New Brunswick. Corrected above; 9780813552378 is the electronic edition.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 👁 VI, 🌐 IX

### Kripal, Jeffrey J. — *Authors of the Impossible: The Paranormal and the Sacred*

Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2011. ISBN 9780226047576. [Publisher](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/A/bo9749373.html)

Kripal reads four figures who took anomalous experience seriously as intellectual subject matter, and argues that trauma often precedes a "noetic break" after which a person's interpretive world is reorganised. Useful for the hermeneutic problem the archive faces constantly: the witness is already interpreting, the analyst is interpreting the interpretation, and neither layer is removable.

`KEY` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🌐 IX

## Gaps

- **Synchronicity.** The Theoretical Framework treats synchronicity as an epiphenomenon of emergent meaning-making (Mission Statement §2.0), and nothing in this file supports that treatment. Jung and Pauli's *The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche* is the obvious primary citation; the apophenia and pattern-detection literature is the obvious counterweight. Currently the project's own theoretical vocabulary outruns its bibliography here.
- **Precognition research and its replication record.** Needed in both directions — the claims and the critiques — if the type is to be defensible in an academic conversation.
- **Base rates for anomalous experience in general populations.** The SPR census (Methodology file) is 1894. Nothing modern.

## Scope

Diffuse phenomena with no identified source: the Hum, infrasound, electromagnetic anomaly, unexplained cold or pressure, persistent geophysical strangeness. Phenomena felt or heard rather than seen, and not attributable to a located object or an implied agent.

## Boundary notes

**⚡ VIII vs 🪑 V** — diffuse versus discrete. A hum with no source, an unlocalised cold, a pressure on the chest → Type VIII. A specific object moving, a located knock → Type V. Both together is common; use `secondary_types`.

**⚡ VIII vs 🛸 III** — Type VIII has no structure and no trajectory. A glow with an edge or a path is Type III.

**⚡ VIII vs 👁 VI** — whether an agent is implied. A sense of *being watched* or of *someone present* is Type VI, however diffuse. Type VIII covers sensation without a sensed other.

**This type's literature is mechanism-heavy, and that asymmetry is itself a finding.** Type VIII is the only type where the conventional-explanation literature is stronger than the anomalistic literature — and one of its anchor papers (French et al. 2009) reports a **negative** result. Methodological agnosticism cuts both ways here. The archive records what the witness experienced; a strong candidate mechanism does not retire the account, and the project does not debrief a witness toward it. See `voice_style_guide.md` §5 — *debunk* is a prohibited speech act in both directions.

Practical consequence: `env_noise` and `env_weather` carry more analytical weight in this type than in any other, and a Type VIII case coded without them is close to unusable.

## Reading order

**Tandy & Lawrence 1998**, then **French et al. 2009** for the controlled test, then **Deming 2004** for the Hum as a reported phenomenon in its own right.

## Infrasound

### Tandy, Vic, and Tony R. Lawrence — "The Ghost in the Machine"

*Journal of the Society for Psychical Research* 62, no. 851 (April 1998): 360–364. ISSN 0037-9751.

The paper that gave Type VIII its mechanism. Tandy traced a persistent sense of presence, peripheral movement, and unease in a laboratory to a standing wave near 19 Hz produced by an extractor fan, and proposed infrasound as a general agent in reports of this kind. Brief, concrete, and the most frequently cited naturalistic account of "haunted" environments anywhere in the literature.

**Moved from** `APS_Biblio_Poltergeist` (v3.0). Details verified against the author's own later reference list and the SPR's index; v3.0's citation was correct.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 🪑 V, 👁 VI

### Tandy, Vic — "Something in the Cellar"

*Journal of the Society for Psychical Research* 64, no. 860 (2000): 129–140.

Tandy's follow-up, applying the infrasound hypothesis to a second site — a cellar in Coventry with a reported presence — and refining the measurement approach. Useful alongside the 1998 paper because it shows the method applied prospectively rather than retrospectively, which is the harder test and the one an APS field protocol would need to meet.

`KEY` · `frame: mechanism`

### French, Christopher C., Usman Haque, Rosie Bunton-Stasyshyn, and Robert Davis — "The 'Haunt' Project: An Attempt to Build a 'Haunted' Room by Manipulating Complex Electromagnetic Fields and Infrasound"

*Cortex* 45, no. 5 (2009): 619–629. ISSN 0010-9452. DOI [10.1016/j.cortex.2007.10.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cortex.2007.10.011).

A purpose-built chamber in which electromagnetic fields and infrasound were manipulated under double-blind conditions while participants reported their experiences. Participants reported a range of anomalous sensations — **but the reports did not track the experimental conditions.** Suggestibility and prior belief predicted the reports; the fields and the infrasound did not.

The most important paper in this file, and the most uncomfortable: it is a well-designed test of the mechanism the two Tandy papers propose, and it does not support it. Holding all three at once, labelled, is what the archive is for. An analyst who cites Tandy without citing French is not representing the literature.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 💭 IV

## The Hum

### Deming, David — "The Hum: An Anomalous Sound Heard Around the World"

*Journal of Scientific Exploration* 18, no. 4 (2004): 571–595. ISSN 0892-3310.

A survey of the Hum as reported across widely separated locations — Taos, Bristol, Largs, Auckland — covering the consistencies in witness description (low-frequency, intermittent, worse indoors and at night, inaudible to most people nearby), the failed attempts to locate a source, and the candidate explanations. The canonical Type VIII case and the clearest demonstration of the type's defining feature: a phenomenon reported consistently by a small fraction of the population in many places, with no established source.

No DOI; JSE did not assign them to 2004 volumes. No official landing page could be confirmed — cite volume, issue and pages.

`CORE` · `frame: anomalistic`

## Gaps

1. **Persinger's "God helmet" work and its replication failures.** The transcranial magnetic stimulation claims and the Swedish replication attempts are directly relevant and entirely absent. Needed in both directions.
2. **Environmental health literature on low-frequency noise and wind-turbine syndrome.** Peer-reviewed, substantial, and about populations reporting exactly the Type VIII symptom set. The single most useful body of work not yet here.
3. **Regional geophysics.** Cascadia sits on an active subduction zone. Microseism, harmonic tremor, and the regional seismic monitoring network are all directly relevant to Type VIII intake, and a correlation check against public seismic data is a genuinely available piece of analysis.
4. **Electromagnetic hypersensitivity** as a reported condition and as a research controversy.
5. **Cold spots and thermal perception** — the most commonly reported Type VIII feature, with nothing here addressing it.

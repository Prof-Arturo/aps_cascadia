## Scope

Lights in the sky, structured craft, drone-like behaviour, and mechanical or hardware failure occurring as part of the event. Also the long argument about whether aerial reports are a modern phenomenon or a modern vocabulary for an old one — which is the argument that matters most to an archive.

## Boundary notes

**🛸 III vs ⚡ VIII** — structure and trajectory. A light with a shape, an edge, or a path is Type III. A diffuse glow, a field effect, or a sound with no locatable source is Type VIII. Where the witness reports both, the identifiable object is primary.

**🛸 III vs 🚪 I** — whether hardware failure is the event or its accompaniment. An engine cutting out *during* a sighting is Type III. A device behaving anomalously as the whole of the report is Type I (digital substrate).

**🛸 III vs 👁 VI** — the occupant problem. Where a report includes both a craft and a figure, the type turns on which the witness's account centres. Vallée's argument below is that this boundary is historically unstable by nature, not through analyst error — which is a reason to code both and let `secondary_types` carry it rather than forcing a choice.

**Register caution.** This type attracts the project's prohibited vocabulary more than any other. In witness-facing material: *aerial phenomenon*, *structured object*, *light*. Not *UFO*, not *craft* unless the witness said it. See `voice_style_guide.md` §3.

## Reading order

**Hynek 1972** for the classification problem, **Vallée 1969** for the continuity thesis, **Pasulka 2019** for where the discourse now sits.

## Classification and method

### Hynek, J. Allen — *The UFO Experience: A Scientific Inquiry*

Chicago: Henry Regnery, 1972. ISBN 9780809291304. OCLC [578213](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/578213).

Hynek — the Air Force's own scientific consultant, writing after two decades of it — sets out a systematic approach to report handling and introduces the Close Encounter classification. Of direct structural interest to the APS: Hynek's categories are built on *observer distance and interaction*, where the APS taxonomy is built on phenomenology. Reading the two against each other clarifies what each buys. The strangeness-versus-credibility matrix is also the nearest ancestor of the project's `oz_factor_flag`.

`CORE` · `frame: method`

## The continuity thesis

### Vallée, Jacques — *Passport to Magonia: From Folklore to Flying Saucers*

Chicago: Henry Regnery, 1969. No ISBN (pre-ISBN imprint). OCLC [44880](https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/44880).
*Reprint:* Brisbane: Daily Grail Publishing, 2014, with a new foreword. ISBN 9780987422484. 373 pp.

Vallée sets modern aerial-encounter reports beside European fairy lore and argues that the two follow one pattern in different vocabularies — abduction, missing time, altered duration, gifts of food, a changed witness. The single most important work in this file for an archive, because it is an argument *about taxonomy*: if the continuity holds, then type boundaries track cultural vocabulary rather than phenomena, and the archive's cross-cutting attributes are doing more work than its types.

**Citation warning.** v3.0 of this bibliography gave Vallée's ISBN as 978-0841503557 — that number belongs to Keel's *Mothman Prophecies* below and was duplicated in error. The 1969 Regnery first edition carries no ISBN, which is correct for its date. Cite the OCLC, or cite the 2014 reprint by its own ISBN. Independently: Google Books' record for the 1969 edition attaches 2014 reprint identifiers to it; do not trust that record.

`CORE` · `frame: anomalistic` · cross-ref → 👁 VI, 🌐 IX, 🐾 II

### Keel, John A. — *The Mothman Prophecies*

New York: Saturday Review Press, 1975. ISBN 9780841503557. OCLC [1250245](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/1250245).

Keel's account of the Point Pleasant events, emphasising synchronicity, absurdity, and the sense of being toyed with. Journalism braided with speculation, and foundational to the high-strangeness register. Operationally relevant for something other than its thesis: Point Pleasant is a textbook **🗺️ Type VII window area**, and Keel's documentation of how a location's reputation accumulated — and how investigators became part of what they documented — is a case study in the contamination problem.

`KEY` · `frame: anomalistic` · cross-ref → 🗺️ VII, 👁 VI

## Psychological and religious framings

### Jung, C. G. — *Flying Saucers: A Modern Myth of Things Seen in the Skies*

Translated by R. F. C. Hull. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 1978. ISBN 9780691018225. OCLC [8805405](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/8805405).

Jung reads the sightings as symbolic projection — a mandala of order appearing during a period of social fracture. Notable for what it is careful *not* to claim: Jung explicitly declines to decide whether anything physical was present, which makes this an unusually clean precedent for methodological agnosticism from an unexpected direction.

`KEY` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → 💭 IV

### Pasulka, Diana Walsh — *American Cosmic: UFOs, Religion, Technology*

New York: Oxford University Press, 2019. ISBN 9780190692889. OCLC [1031402183](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/1031402183).

Pasulka examines contemporary belief through religious studies, arguing that these narratives function as an emerging spiritual framework, and draws on ethnographic work with scientists and experiencers who hold professional reputations at risk. The most useful recent citation for a partner conversation, because it models serious academic engagement without adjudication — which is the posture the project is asked to defend most often.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🌐 IX

## Gaps

- **Conventional-cause baseline.** Nothing here covers satellite re-entry, Starlink trains, high-altitude balloons, lenticular cloud, or astronomical misidentification. Type III has the widest gap between its reported volume and its mechanism literature, and this omission is operationally costly: the Cascadia launch corridor and heavy regional air traffic make ordinary explanations common, and an analyst who cannot name them cannot responsibly code a residual.
- **Official record.** No citation to the Condon Report, Project Blue Book, or the post-2017 AARO and UAP Task Force material. Whatever their merits, they are what a journalist will ask about.
- **Witness reliability under night-sky conditions.** Angular size, distance, and motion estimation against an empty field. Relevant to Type III and ⚡ VIII alike.

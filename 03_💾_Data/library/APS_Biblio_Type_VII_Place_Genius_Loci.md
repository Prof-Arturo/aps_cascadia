## Scope

Locations that accumulate unusual reports across many witnesses over time: window areas, sacred sites, stretches of road, places with multi-generational histories.

Type VII is the only type whose unit of analysis is **not an experience**. It is a *place*, and the evidence for it is a body of accounts rather than any single one. That makes it structurally different from every other type in the taxonomy, and it has a coding consequence stated below.

## Boundary notes

**The threshold rule.** A single powerful experience at a location is **🚪 Type I**, not Type VII — even a vivid one, even at a place with a reputation. Type VII requires **multiple independent witnesses, or a documented multi-generational record.** Without that, there is no place-level claim to make, only an event.

In practice this means Type VII is assigned by an analyst reviewing the archive, rarely by intake. A witness cannot report a Type VII case; they report a Type I, II, V, VI or VIII case that an analyst later recognises as belonging to a cluster. The intake instrument is right not to solicit it directly.

**Independence matters.** Ostension and local reputation produce clusters that are transmission artefacts rather than place-level patterns. `res_id` exists to make clusters visible; it does not establish that the reports are independent. Dégh & Vázsonyi (Methodology file) is the necessary caution, and Keel's Point Pleasant documentation (🛸 III) is the worked example of an investigator becoming part of the cluster.

**`indigenous_territory` always applies.** Every Type VII site sits on land with a prior history of meaning, named or unnamed in the reports. See 🌐 Type IX before any site-level publication.

## Reading order

**Norberg-Schulz 1980** for the concept the type is named after, **Tuan 1977** for the phenomenology, **Bell 1997** for the mechanism of place-attachment, **Persinger & Lafrenière 1977** for the window-area claim and its problems.

## The concept

### Norberg-Schulz, Christian — *Genius Loci: Towards a Phenomenology of Architecture*

New York: Rizzoli, 1980. ISBN 9780847802876. 213 pp.

The work the type is named for. Norberg-Schulz adapts the Roman *genius loci* — the presiding spirit of a place — into an architectural phenomenology in which a place has a character that can be identified, respected, or destroyed by building. The term the APS uses for Type VII comes from here, and citing it correctly matters: the project's usage is Norberg-Schulz's descriptive sense, **not** a claim that a site is inhabited.

Note a UK edition (Academy Editions, London) exists alongside the Rizzoli printing.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural`

### Tuan, Yi-Fu — *Space and Place: The Perspective of Experience*

Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 1977. OCLC [3126299](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/3126299). vi + 235 pp.
ISBNs recorded for the 1977 printing: 9780816608089 and 9780816608843 — binding qualifiers not confirmed. 25th anniversary ed. (2001): 9780816638772.

Tuan's distinction between **space** (abstract, undifferentiated extent) and **place** (space invested with meaning through experience) is the vocabulary Type VII needs. Space becomes place through dwelling, attention, and repetition — which is also an account of how a site accumulates a reputation, and so of how Type VII clusters form whatever their cause.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🚪 I

### Bell, Michael Mayerfeld — "The Ghosts of Place"

*Theory and Society* 26 (1997): 813–836. DOI [10.1023/A:1006888230610](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1006888230610).

Bell argues that the felt presence of those who have been somewhere before — the dead, former residents, prior uses — is a constitutive feature of how people experience place, not an exotic addition to it. A sociological account of why places feel occupied that neither asserts nor denies occupancy. The closest thing in the scholarly literature to the project's own posture on Type VII, and the single most quotable citation for a partner or journalist conversation about this type.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 👁 VI

## The window-area claim

### Persinger, Michael A., and Gyslaine F. Lafrenière — *Space-Time Transients and Unusual Events*

Chicago: Nelson-Hall, 1977. ISBN-10 0882293346. OCLC [2151248](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/2151248). xiii + 267 pp.

The origin of the **window area** idea in its quantitative form: Persinger and Lafrenière compiled some six thousand reports and argued for geographic clustering correlated with tectonic strain, proposing a geophysical mechanism. Foundational to Type VII because it is the first attempt to treat place-level clustering as a measurable claim rather than a local tradition.

Flagged as `CONTESTED` for method rather than for ambition: the source data were press and catalogue reports with uncontrolled collection, so population density, media coverage, and reporting infrastructure are not separated from the signal. That critique is directly instructive for the APS — it is the same confound the project's own regional coverage will produce, and the reason a Type VII cluster needs independence established before it means anything. Read as a cautionary methodological precedent as much as a substantive claim.

`CONTESTED` · `frame: mechanism` · cross-ref → ⚡ VIII, 🛸 III

## Gaps

1. **Cultural geography of the sacred site** — pilgrimage, the management of contested sites, how sanctity is maintained and transmitted.
2. **Environmental psychology of place attachment** — the empirical literature behind Tuan's phenomenology.
3. **Cascadia-specific regional history.** The most practical gap: Region 10's own documented sites, their report histories, and their land histories. This is archival and local-history work rather than a purchase, and it is the natural first commission for a regional research associate.
4. **Geophysical correlates after Persinger.** Subsequent attempts to test the tectonic-strain hypothesis, including the negative results.
5. **Deep-mapping and counter-mapping method** — how to document a site's accumulated meaning without flattening it, which is a methodological need the project will hit as soon as it publishes site-level material.

## Scope

Interpretive frameworks in which reality is information, code, or representation — and the vernacular religious forms that grow around that premise. Simulation theory, hyperreality, techno-mysticism, "glitch" discourse.

## This is not a type

**There is no `TYPE_X` for simulation, and there should not be.** This file holds *frameworks witnesses use*, not a category of experience. Under the v4.1 taxonomy:

- A witness reporting that reality glitched, that a device responded to thought, or that a scene re-rendered → **🚪 Type I**, with `substrate` carrying the digital distinction.
- A witness who holds simulation as a cosmology within which they interpret an experience → **🌐 Type IX**, with `cultural_frame_flag` set. Type IX is not only Indigenous or traditional; a techno-mystical frame is a living cosmological frame and is coded as one.

This file exists so an analyst can recognise the vocabulary and cite its sources. Its contents are the `narrative_frame` of a growing share of intake, and they belong in the library for the same reason the Backrooms material does: a framework the witness arrives with is data about transmission.

## Register caution

This is the most theoretically loaded file in the library, and the Iceberg Method applies strictly. *Hyperreality*, *ontologically prior*, *informational substrate* are internal vocabulary. Nothing here should surface in a witness-facing document. See `voice_style_guide.md` §2.

## Reading order

**Baudrillard 1994** for the framework, **Bostrom 2003** for the argument in its analytic form, **Davis 2015** for how it became a vernacular religion.

## Representation and the real

### Baudrillard, Jean — *Simulacra and Simulation*

Translated by Sheila Faria Glaser. Ann Arbor: University of Michigan Press, 1994. ISBN 9780472065219. OCLC [856796350](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/856796350).

Baudrillard's argument that media-saturated cultures substitute simulation for the real, until the copy precedes and determines the original. The theoretical root of glitch and simulation discourse, and — more usefully for an archive — an account of *why* witnesses increasingly reach for representational metaphors to describe anomalous experience. When a witness says the scene looked rendered, this is the lineage of that word.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🚪 I

### Chalmers, David J. — *Reality+: Virtual Worlds and the Problems of Philosophy*

New York: W. W. Norton, 2022. ISBN 9780393635805.

Chalmers argues that virtual worlds are genuine realities and that simulated experience is not thereby less real. Moves the simulation question away from scepticism about whether we can know, toward what would follow if we did. Relevant because it supplies the strongest *philosophically serious* version of a framework witnesses hold in a popular version — useful when the project is asked whether it takes such frames seriously.

`KEY` · `frame: anomalistic`

## The simulation argument

### Bostrom, Nick — "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?"

*Philosophical Quarterly* 53, no. 211 (2003): 243–255. DOI [10.1111/1467-9213.00309](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9213.00309).

The trilemma: at least one of (a) civilisations go extinct before reaching simulation capability, (b) they decline to run ancestor simulations, or (c) we are almost certainly in one. Cited constantly and read rarely — worth reading, because Bostrom's actual claim is disjunctive and considerably more modest than the popular version witnesses will have encountered. Knowing the difference is useful in a debriefing where a witness invokes it.

`CORE` · `frame: anomalistic`

### Wheeler, John Archibald — "Information, Physics, Quantum: The Search for Links"

In *Proceedings of the 3rd International Symposium on Foundations of Quantum Mechanics*, 354–368. Tokyo: Physical Society of Japan, 1989. [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/WHEIPQ)

"It from bit" — Wheeler's proposal that every physical quantity derives its meaning from binary yes-or-no registrations. A serious physicist's informational reading of physical reality, and the citation most often reached for in support of simulation framings.

Flagged for care in both directions: Wheeler's claim is about measurement and information-theoretic foundations, and he did not argue that reality is a computation in the sense popular discourse takes him to. Cite him for what he said.

`KEY` · `frame: mechanism`

## Techno-mysticism as vernacular religion

### Davis, Erik — *TechGnosis: Myth, Magic, and Mysticism in the Age of Information*

Updated ed. Berkeley: North Atlantic Books, 2015. ISBN 9781583949306. OCLC [55656246](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/55656246).

Davis traces how technology and esoteric belief have co-evolved since the telegraph, and how metaphors of code, circuitry, signal, and network become vessels for religious interpretation. **The most important work in this file for the APS**, because it is the one that connects the framework to the taxonomy: it establishes simulation discourse as a vernacular religious form, which is what justifies coding a simulation-framed account as 🌐 Type IX rather than treating it as a merely fashionable metaphor.

Read alongside Primiano on vernacular religion (📐 PROTOCOL). The two together are the argument.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🌐 IX, 🚪 I

### Virk, Rizwan — *The Simulation Hypothesis*

San Francisco: Bayview Books, 2019. ISBN 9780983056904.

A popular synthesis of scientific, spiritual, and cultural strands of simulation discourse. Retained for a specific and limited purpose: this register — rather than Bostrom's or Chalmers's — is what most witnesses invoking simulation will have encountered, and the library should contain the version actually in circulation. Read as a document of the discourse rather than as an argument within it; the physics and the Eastern-philosophy parallels are not load-bearing.

`CONTESTED` · `frame: anomalistic`

## Gaps

- **Glitch-in-the-Matrix narrative as a folkloric form.** The Reddit corpus is large, the scholarship is thin, and it is the single most common digital-native frame in current intake. The nearest analogue in the library is the Backrooms scholarship in 🚪 Type I; an equivalent for glitch narrative does not yet exist and may be a paper the project could write rather than cite.
- **Mandela-effect and collective false-memory discourse.** Adjacent, frequently invoked, and connected to McNally on memory reconstruction (💭 IV).
- **Digital animism and the attribution of agency to systems** — relevant as witnesses increasingly report conversational AI and recommender systems behaving as though aware of them, which is live Type I substrate territory.
- **Critique of the simulation argument.** The file currently carries the argument and its popularisation but none of its rebuttals, which is an imbalance the other files would not tolerate.

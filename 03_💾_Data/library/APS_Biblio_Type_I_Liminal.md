## Scope

Spatial distortion, missing time, threshold crossing, and places that do not quite fit — the road that repeats, the corridor that is longer coming back, the building whose interior will not reconcile with its exterior.

**Digital-substrate cases fall here** per the v4.1 taxonomy: feeds, devices, or interfaces behaving as though responsive to attention or thought. The `substrate` attribute carries the physical-versus-digital distinction, so it does not need a type of its own. This consolidation is why the Type I outreach package is titled *Liminal / Digital*.

## Boundary notes

**🚪 I vs 🗺️ VII** — the distinction is event versus site. A witness reporting that *a place behaved wrongly on an occasion* is Type I. A *location accumulating reports across many witnesses over time* is Type VII. The same stretch of road generates both, and a Type I case at a known Type VII site should carry the site in `res_id`. A single powerful experience at a reputed location is still Type I.

**🚪 I vs 💭 IV** — where the witness locates the anomaly. Missing time experienced as a gap in one's own awareness is Type IV; missing time experienced as the road having repeated is Type I. When the witness is uncertain, the account's own spatial language usually decides it, and the Grimm Protocol says to follow the witness's language rather than resolve it for them.

**Ostension caution** — Backrooms and liminal-space aesthetics are now widely circulated, and a witness who arrived at a site through that repertoire may describe a genuine experience in borrowed vocabulary. Code the experience; record the vocabulary in `narrative_frame`. See Dégh & Vázsonyi in the Methodology file.

## Reading order

**Augé 1995**, then **Freud 1919**, then **Cho & Underberg-Goode 2025** for the contemporary digital form.

## Theoretical foundations

### Augé, Marc — *Non-Places: Introduction to an Anthropology of Supermodernity*

Translated by John Howe. London: Verso, 1995. ISBN 9781859840511. OCLC [32968908](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/32968908).

Augé's **non-place** — the transitory, anonymous, instruction-governed space produced by late modernity: the airport concourse, the motorway services, the hotel corridor. Non-places are defined by what cannot happen in them: no relation, no history, no identity. The theoretical backdrop for nearly every Type I report, and the reason the type's reports cluster so heavily in infrastructure rather than in wilderness.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural` · cross-ref → 🗺️ VII

### Freud, Sigmund — "The Uncanny"

In *The Standard Edition of the Complete Psychological Works of Sigmund Freud*, vol. 17, edited by James Strachey, 217–256. London: Hogarth Press, 1955. Originally published 1919. OCLC [223710](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/223710).

The *unheimlich* as the unfamiliar emerging within the familiar — dread produced not by strangeness but by strangeness in a setting that should be safe. Explains why Type I reports so consistently involve mundane settings rather than dramatic ones, and why witnesses describe the affect as recognition rather than surprise.

`CORE` · `frame: mechanism`

## Digital substrate

### Cho, Day, and Natalie Underberg-Goode — "Lost in Liminal Space: The Internet Legend of the Backrooms"

*Contemporary Legend* 3 (May 2025): 16–35. [Article](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/cl/article/view/41598) · [PDF](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/cl/article/download/41598/43424/121004)

Reads the Backrooms as digital legend-tripping conducted through participatory culture, and liminal horror as collective processing of displacement and grief. The most current scholarly treatment, and the right citation when a journalist asks why these reports increased after 2020.

`CORE` · `frame: cultural`

### Wiggins, Bradley Earl — "The Backrooms and Liminal Spaces: Explorations of a Digital Urban Legend"

*New Media & Society* 27 (2024): 4729–4747. DOI [10.1177/14614448241238395](https://doi.org/10.1177/14614448241238395).

Examines how low resolution, default textures, and architectural banality *increase* believability rather than diminishing it. Operationally useful: the same logic governs witness photographs, and an analyst should not treat poor image quality as diminished credibility.

`KEY` · `frame: cultural`

### Pixels, Kane (Kane Parsons) — *The Backrooms (Found Footage)*

YouTube video, 9:15. 7 January 2022. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H4dGpz6cnHo

The short film that moved the Backrooms from a single image-board post to a cinematic form, and initiated the analog-horror wave that followed. Held as a **primary source**: it is not scholarship about the phenomenon, it is a substantial part of the repertoire witnesses now draw on. Cite accordingly.

`PRIMARY SOURCE` · `frame: cultural`

### Yalcinkaya, Günseli — "Inside the Uncanny World of #liminalspaces TikTok"

*Dazed Digital*, 14 April 2021. https://www.dazeddigital.com/science-tech/article/52477/1/inside-the-uncanny-world-of-liminal-spaces-tiktok-reddit-nostalgia

Links architectural theory and postmodern placelessness to the platform aesthetics that carried them to a mass audience. Journalism rather than scholarship, retained because it documents the moment of transmission and because its framing is roughly the framing witnesses arrive with.

`JOURNALISM` · `frame: cultural`

## Gaps

- **Spatial disorientation and wayfinding.** The conventional-mechanism literature for Type I is absent. Research on route memory, vection, and the conditions under which familiar routes are misjudged would let an analyst recognise the ordinary cases without dismissing the accounts. Currently the type has strong cultural scholarship and no mechanism scholarship, which is an imbalance the other direction from ⚡ Type VIII.
- **Time perception.** Missing-time reports are frequent in Cascadia intake and nothing here addresses duration estimation.
- **Pre-digital liminal tradition.** Fairy-path, boundary, and threshold motifs long predate non-place theory. Vallée 1969 (🛸 III) touches the continuity; no dedicated citation covers it.

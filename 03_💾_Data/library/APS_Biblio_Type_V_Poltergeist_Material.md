## Scope

Physical interaction with the environment: objects displaced, knocks and impacts, apports, marks and traces, doors and switches. The parapsychological literature calls the pattern **RSPK** (recurrent spontaneous psychokinesis) and the folkloric literature calls it poltergeist; the APS uses neither term with witnesses.

## Boundary notes

**🪑 V vs ⚡ VIII** — discrete versus diffuse. A specific object moving, a located knock, a mark on a surface is Type V. A field effect, a hum, an unlocalised cold or pressure is Type VIII. Both in one report is common and should use `secondary_types`.

**🪑 V vs 👁 VI** — material interaction with no figure present is Type V. A figure present alongside the interaction is **Type VI primary, V secondary.** The entity, where reported, governs.

**🪑 V is partly a witness-configuration question.** Roll's focal-agent model holds that these cases centre on a person rather than a place — which means Type V is the type where `witness_configuration` carries the most analytical weight, and where a case coded without it is substantially less useful. Note the ethical edge: the focal-agent literature historically centred adolescents in households under strain, and an analyst should be alert to what a report may be carrying. The project documents; it does not diagnose, and it does not relay a family's circumstances into a public record. `community_context` (column AO) is never published.

## Reading order

**Gauld & Cornell 1979** for the case corpus, **Roll 1972** for the focal-agent model, **Houran & Lange 2001** for the competing explanations side by side.

## The scholarly corpus

### Gauld, Alan, and A. D. Cornell — *Poltergeists*

London: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1979. ISBN 9780710001856. OCLC [4665732](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/4665732).

Five hundred cases from the sixteenth century onward, subjected to cluster analysis and sorted by reported feature. The closest thing to a canonical academic reference on the subject, and the methodological ancestor of what the APS registry is for: Gauld and Cornell's finding that the corpus separates into distinguishable clusters is an argument that taxonomy can be derived from a case body rather than imposed on it.

`CORE` · `frame: anomalistic`

### Roll, William G. — *The Poltergeist*

New York: New American Library, 1972. ISBN 9780451141508. OCLC [977594](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/977594).

Introduces **RSPK** and the **focal agent**, reframing these cases as centred on a living person rather than on an external presence or a location. Whatever one concludes about the mechanism, the reframing is the reason the APS records `witness_configuration` as a cross-cutting attribute rather than a per-type field.

`CORE` · `frame: anomalistic`

### Houran, James, and Rense Lange, eds. — *Hauntings and Poltergeists: Multidisciplinary Perspectives*

Jefferson, NC: McFarland, 2001. ISBN 9780786410227. OCLC [44502478](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/44502478).

Psychological, sociological, environmental, and physical accounts collected in one volume without the editors adjudicating between them. The most directly useful book in this file for an analyst, because the structure *is* methodological agnosticism — competing explanations presented at equal weight for the reader to hold open. If the project ever needs to show a partner what its posture looks like in published form, this is the example.

`CORE` · `frame: method` · cross-ref → ⚡ VIII, 🗺️ VII

## Case documentation

### Playfair, Guy Lyon — *This House Is Haunted: The True Story of the Enfield Poltergeist*

London: Souvenir Press, 1980. ISBN 9780285625293. OCLC [7175512](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/7175512).

The Enfield case at length, including auditory phenomena and claimed levitation. Contested in its specifics — some phenomena were acknowledged by the children as produced — and retained for exactly that reason: Enfield is the best-documented instance of the problem that admitted fabrication within a case does not by itself settle the rest of it, and of how investigator presence shapes what a case becomes.

`CONTESTED` · `frame: anomalistic`

### Price, Harry — *Poltergeist: Tales of the Supernatural*

London: Bracken Books, 1993. ISBN 9781858910840. Originally published as *Poltergeist Over England: Three Centuries of Mischievous Ghosts* (London: Country Life, 1945).

Price's survey of British cases, significant for documenting pre-war outbreaks that would otherwise be unrecorded. Price's own methods drew sustained criticism, including allegations of fabrication at Borley; cite the case descriptions, not the conclusions, and prefer the 1945 title when citing the original.

`CONTESTED` · `frame: anomalistic`

### Radin, Dean — *Entangled Minds: Extrasensory Experiences in a Quantum Reality*

New York: Paraview Pocket Books, 2006. ISBN 9781416516774. OCLC [61116728](https://www.worldcat.org/oclc/61116728).

Radin argues that psi findings may be compatible with nonlocality in quantum mechanics. Retained as a **documented position within the field**, flagged clearly: the physics appeal is rejected by most working physicists, and the meta-analytic claims are disputed on method. An archive that holds the naturalistic literature at face value should hold the advocacy literature at face value too — labelled, in both directions, which is what agnosticism requires in practice.

`CONTESTED` · `frame: anomalistic`

## Gaps

- **Fraud and sleight of hand.** No citation on the conjuring literature or on how object-displacement effects are produced deliberately. Type V is the type most readily staged, and the file currently gives an analyst no basis for recognising it. The most serious gap here.
- **Structural and acoustic causes.** Settling, thermal expansion, plumbing hammer, HVAC cycling, pest activity — the ordinary causes of knocks and movement in houses. Nothing covers them.
- **The focal-agent literature after Roll.** Fifty years of subsequent work, including the critiques, is unrepresented.

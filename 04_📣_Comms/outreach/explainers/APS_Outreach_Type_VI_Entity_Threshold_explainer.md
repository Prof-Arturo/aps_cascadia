# The Someone Who Was Not There

*A public explainer from the American Periphenomenal Survey*

---

There are figures who show up in the accounts of otherwise reliable witnesses and cannot be explained away. Someone standing at the foot of the bed at three in the morning. A grandmother in the doorway of the kitchen, months after her funeral. A shape at the edge of the porch that felt like a person but did not have a face. A shadow on the wall that seemed to have intention. The certainty that someone was in the room, so complete you turned around, and no one was there.

These are among the oldest reports in the folklore record. They also remain among the most common contemporary reports, in every part of the world where people have been asked. Most of the people who see something like this do not talk about it. When they do, they are typically not believed. They typically stop trying.

The American Periphenomenal Survey collects these accounts.

## What we are collecting

Reports of figures, presences, and visitations that could not be explained by the ordinary presence of another person. This includes:

- **Figures in the room** — someone standing at the bedside, in the doorway, at the edge of vision, seen briefly and then gone. Sometimes present in enough detail to describe the clothes; sometimes only a shape.
- **Shadow presences** — dark shapes that felt like people, without clear features, most often at the edge of a room or the edge of a treeline.
- **Ancestral visitations** — a parent, grandparent, spouse, or long-dead relative who appeared in a way that felt more than memory. Sometimes silent. Sometimes speaking. Sometimes the message is what stayed.
- **Threshold-state encounters** — figures seen in the moments between waking and sleep, during high fever, near a life-threatening event, or during deep meditation. Distinct from dreams; the witness knows the difference.
- **Presences without form** — the total certainty that someone was in the room, felt with the same clarity you feel another person's presence in ordinary life, without seeing anything at all.

## Why we collect them

Encounters like these have been documented by folklorists, physicians, anthropologists, and — where the culture supports it — by religious and spiritual traditions for as long as records have been kept. They are not confined to any one culture, tradition, class of witness, or historical era. They are among the most cross-culturally consistent phenomena in the human record.

They are also, in most contemporary Western contexts, treated as either evidence of a fringe belief system or as symptoms to be explained away. Neither approach has helped researchers understand what witnesses are actually reporting.

Our approach is different. We take the account seriously as an account — as a specific event that happened to a specific person, described in the specific language that person reaches for. We do not try to decide whether the figure was "real" in any particular sense. We record what the witness saw, felt, and did next. Across many such reports, patterns emerge — patterns about *when* these encounters happen, *where*, *to whom*, and *under what conditions*. Those patterns are what we are here for.

## A note on the difference between this and Type II

Some encounters involve unknown biological creatures — figures with tracks, vocalizations, physical evidence of biological presence. Those belong to a different category of our archive (**Type II — Cryptid & Biological**). What distinguishes this category is the absence of biological evidence: the figures leave no tracks, produce no sound with a physical source, and are typically seen once and never again in a form that could be documented.

If your experience involved evidence of a biological creature — footprints, calls, scat, hair samples, or the presence of an animal-shaped body at close range — that is a different kind of report, and we are also collecting those.

## What happens when you submit

Your account is added to the Cascadia Archive. It is coded by an analyst according to what you described — the setting, the timing, whether you were alone or with others, what state you were in (fully awake, drowsy, feverish, in grief, in meditation), whether the figure resembled anyone or anything you recognized, and what you did next. Your contact information is kept separate and never published.

You choose whether your account appears in the public registry (identifying information removed) or is held for internal research only. This is your decision, confirmed at the time of submission and revisable later.

If your account is unusual, or connects to a pattern of reports we are tracking, we may ask to follow up with a longer conversation. You can decline; most submissions do not lead to a follow-up.

## Submit

If you have seen someone who was not there — recently, or years ago, or many times — we would like to hear from you.

# American Periphenomenal Survey

A citizen science and vernacular research initiative documenting anomalous and high-strangeness experiences across North America.

## What this is

The APS is, at its heart, a storytelling project. We intend to collect, and eventually tell, the anomalous experience stories of the people who live on this continent. The data infrastructure supporting that work exists to make those stories tellable: queryable, comparable, analytically tractable, preservable. However, it is not meant to reduce those stories to mere data points. Rather, it is meant to add extra textures and dimensions to the storytelling, hopefully making it a more immersive experience.

The project operates at the intersection of three established fields without being fully contained by any of them: **anomalistics** (the scientific study of anomalous phenomena), **humanistic psychology** (particularly the narrative and mythic dimensions of experience), and **experience-centered folklore studies** (the Hufford tradition of taking vernacular anomalous experience accounts seriously on their own terms, prior to cultural interpretation).

Our methodological commitment is **methodological agnosticism**; we do not debunk, and we do not prove. We observe, collect, and archive.

The word *periphenomena* refers to experiences at the edge of the phenomenal; real and reportable, occurring adjacent to and continuous with ordinary experience without being fully accommodated by conventional scientific, psychological, or cultural explanatory frameworks. Not *paranormal* (which claims occult causation), not *supernatural* (which claims transcendence of nature), not *epiphenomenal* (which claims causal irrelevance), not merely *anomalous* — *adjacent*. The Mission Statement §2.0 develops the full distinction.

For the full philosophical and methodological framework, see [`01_✒️ Admin/APS_Mission_Statement_Reg10_Cascadia.docx`](01_✒️_Admin/APS_Mission_Statement_Reg10_Cascadia.docx).

## Regional structure

The APS is organized into ten bioregional units. Region 10 (Cascadia) is the founding and currently active unit. The other nine regional frameworks are documented but not yet operational.

| #  | Unit         | Domain                                       | Status     |

| 02 | Appalachian  | The Ancient Mountain Spine                   | Planned    |

| 04 | Laurentian   | The Freshwater Seas                          | Planned    |
| 05 | Heartland    | Great Plains & Prairies                      | Planned    |

## Repository structure

This repository contains the project's public-facing materials and infrastructure, organized by departmental ownership:

- **`01_✒️_Admin/`** — Canonical project documents (Mission Statement, Operations Manual, Field Manual, Project State, Changelog)
- **`02_📋_Field/`** — Field collection tools (Field Instrument, Field Questionnaire, Resource Card, Debriefing Protocol)
- **`03_💾_Data/`** — Data layer specifications (Master Data Protocol, schemas, submission handler source)
- **`04_📣_Comms/`** — Visual identity, outreach materials, social media, voice style guide

The intake form (`index.html`) and runtime web assets (`media/`) live at the repository root and are deployed to the public website via GitHub Pages.

## Data separation

Witness narratives, contact information, and the live submission database are **not** stored in this repository. They live in private Google Drive folders with access restricted to the project's principal investigator. See [`03_💾_Data/data_storage_architecture.md`](03_database/data_storage_architecture.md) for the full architecture.

This split is deliberate. Schema and infrastructure are open for inspection. Witness data is not.
